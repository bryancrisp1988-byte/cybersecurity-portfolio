# Module Review — Module 1: Introduction to IT

## Overview
Module 1 introduced the foundational concepts of Information Technology. It explored how computing began, how modern systems are structured, and the essential responsibilities of IT support professionals. This module builds the baseline knowledge required for understanding hardware, operating systems, networking, and cybersecurity later in the course.

## What I Learned

### 1. Intro to IT Support
- The role of IT support as the first point of contact for technical issues.
- Importance of communication, documentation, and customer service.
- Basic troubleshooting workflow: identify → diagnose → resolve → document.
- How IT support contributes to system stability and security.

### 2. History of Computing
- Evolution from early mechanical devices to digital computers.
- Key innovators such as Ada Lovelace, Charles Babbage, and Alan Turing.
- Transition from mainframes to personal computers and modern operating systems.
- How historical limitations shaped today’s computing standards.

### 3. Digital Logic
- Binary representation and how computers store/process information.
- Logic gates (AND, OR, NOT, XOR) and truth tables.
- How circuits make decisions and form the basis of CPU operations.
- Why understanding digital logic helps with hardware troubleshooting.

### 4. Computer Architecture Layers
- Hardware → Operating System → Applications → Networking.
- How abstraction simplifies complex systems.
- How layers interact and depend on each other.
- Why layered architecture is important for diagnosing issues and securing systems.

## Skills Gained
- Ability to read and understand binary and basic digital logic.
- Awareness of how computer systems evolved and why modern systems work the way they do.
- Understanding of layered architecture and how components interact.
- Foundational troubleshooting mindset and documentation habits.
- Clear understanding of IT support responsibilities and workflows.

## How This Module Supports Cybersecurity Learning
- Digital logic explains how vulnerabilities can occur at the hardware level.
- Architecture layers show where attacks target (OS, applications, network).
- IT support workflows mirror real incident response processes.
- Historical context explains why legacy systems and protocols still exist in modern environments.

## Personal Reflection
This module gave me a strong foundation in how computers operate at every level — from binary logic to full operating systems. It helped me understand not just *how* systems work, but *why* they behave the way they do. I feel more prepared to move into hardware, networking, and cybersecurity topics with confidence.
